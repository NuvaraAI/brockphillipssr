AI² & Writing Studio

Learning and writing workflows built around original attempts, source evidence, reflection, version history, and human review.

AI-assisted learning should leave the learner able to explain the work. AI² and Writing Studio approach that problem through original attempts, revisions, source evidence, reflection, and human review.

My contribution

I shaped the learning workflow, the expectations for reasoning and attribution, and the product requirements. I directed AI-assisted construction and review of the materials, application behavior, and acceptance records.

Writing Studio implementation

  • Assignment rules that distinguish permitted AI assistance from manual work.
  • Evidence and source notes connected to a writing task.
  • Reflection questions about reasoning, alternatives, and transfer.
  • Numbered version saves, conflict detection, export, and attribution.
  • Cancellation and pause controls around assisted work.

The standalone Writing Studio uses a dependency-free Node server. Its October 8, 2026 readiness record reports 15 real-server checks of non-AI behavior. The actual local-model workflow was held by a disk-resource guard, so these checks do not establish successful model-assisted learning.

Broader learning-system work

Related Cyber learning packages connect lessons, objective units, practical tasks, simulations, review rubrics, and reporting. The inspected September package includes 14 catalog pathways and preserves the distinction between course materials, official exam coverage, and release acceptance.

The work also includes role controls, completion prerequisites, and evidence-preserving review. Authoring a course and validating it for learners are distinct deliverables.

Evidence and status

The current public Nuvara homepage describes a limited AI² pilot. The latest private full-journey record found in this review, dated October 8, still contains unresolved parent-workspace/session acceptance and unreleased repair work. The portfolio therefore claims implemented components and documented learning design, without claiming a fully accepted learner journey or measured learning gains.

The Cyber candidate's inspected readiness report records unpassed hosted and independent instructional release gates. It does not establish a released certification program.

Skills demonstrated: education product design, versioned evidence, source attribution, API/session review, acceptance criteria, honest readiness reporting.
