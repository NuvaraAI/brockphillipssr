Public case studies summarize that evidence. Original source code, raw conversation exports, internal audits, network identifiers, client files, and private operating records remain outside the repository. A summary is not a substitute for access to those underlying materials.

## What the status labels mean

- **Published / public website live:** a current public artifact was observed.
- **Implemented / working local behavior:** code and recorded local checks support the described behavior within a specific scope.
- **Retained test result:** the original dated report was inspected; its tests were not rerun for this portfolio.
- **Prototype / pilot / in development:** artifacts exist, with acceptance, integration, or release work still open.
- **Research / proposal:** writing or planning exists; funding, implementation, peer review, and benefit require further evidence.

Counts stay attached to their scope. Registered workers are not completed jobs, programming entries are not finished episodes, and authored materials are not validated learner outcomes.

## Training and authorship

Technology course certificates are completion records, not evidence of passing separate CompTIA, AWS, or Microsoft certification exams. Recent technology coursework involved AI assistance; these records are not an independent mastery assessment.

Military and civilian training entries retain their historical context. VMET reporting windows are identified when an exact completion day is unavailable. Duplicate and repeated annual entries are consolidated where appropriate. A completed historical CPR, first aid, or other course does not establish current certification. Transcript recommendations do not establish awarded college credit.

Software and document work also used AI assistance. My contribution includes purpose, product requirements, architecture, direction, review, corrections, and decisions. The portfolio does not claim that every line of code or prose was produced without assistance.

## Shared-certificate treatment

Shared copies preserve the relevant issuer, recipient, course, and completion information. Personal identifiers, private administrative details, and printed certificate identifiers are removed where present, filenames are normalized, and document metadata is stripped. The untouched originals remain private.

These are redacted copies of issuer records, not replacement credentials, AI-generated certificates, or live issuer verification links.
